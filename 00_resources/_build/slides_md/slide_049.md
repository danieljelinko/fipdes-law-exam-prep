### BSE Report & Inquiry

**Southwood Report (1989)** — concluded BSE is unlikely to pose a threat to humans. Recommended setting up an expert committee to advise on SE research.
*(Ref: Report of the Working Party on Bovine Spongiform Encephalopathy, Department of Health, Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food, 1989 — Southwood Report.)*

On **22 December 1997**, Minister of Agriculture Jack Cunningham announced that an inquiry would be undertaken to review the emergence of BSE and vCJD and the adequacy of responses to the disease. The inquiry followed the widespread belief that the government and its scientific advisers had handled the emergence of BSE badly and that public trust had been undermined.

**The 2000 BSE Inquiry Report** points out that the most serious failure of the UK Government was one of **risk communication**. The government's failure to communicate the risks BSE posed to humans can be traced back to a lack of transparency in the first risk assessment by the Southwood Working Party.

The report was critical of the way the crisis had been handled:

- Government was acknowledged to have been well-meaning, but its **lack of transparency, delay in taking action, and desire not to overreact** contributed to failures.
- The escalation of BSE into a crisis was the result of **intensive farming and herbivore cows being fed cow and sheep remains**.
- The crisis was exacerbated by delays, bureaucracy and lack of rigour of officials in considering how policy should be turned into practice.
- The adamant reassurance over the safety of meat was considered a significant error that undermined public confidence.

*Ref:* Report to the Minister of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food and the Secretary of State for Health — *The BSE Inquiry: The Report* (London, The Stationery Office, 2001).
