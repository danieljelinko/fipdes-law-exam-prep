## Q1. The Hygiene Package and Regulation 852/2004

**Topic area:** Hygiene Package
**Difficulty:** core (explanatory)
**Time budget:** 1 hour

### Question prompt

> Explain the purpose and main principles of the EU Hygiene Package. In particular, discuss the role of Regulation (EC) No 852/2004 in imposing general hygiene obligations on food business operators.

### Answer outline

1. **Opening:** The Hygiene Package is the set of EU hygiene rules adopted in 2004 and applicable from 1 January 2006, designed to harmonise and simplify scattered EU hygiene directives.
2. **Main body:**
   - Core instruments: Reg (EC) No 852/2004 for all foodstuffs; Reg (EC) No 853/2004 for foods of animal origin; Reg (EC) No 854/2004 for official controls, later repealed and replaced by Reg (EU) 2017/625.
   - Main principles: farm-to-fork hygiene policy; primary responsibility on FBOs; traceability; HACCP-based FSMS; food-handler training.
   - Reg 852/2004 applies generally to all food businesses, with Annex I for primary production and Annex II for general hygiene requirements.
   - Annex II covers premises, equipment, waste, water supply, personal hygiene, temperature control, training and related hygiene duties.
   - Article 6 requires registration of food establishments and, where required by other law, approval.
3. **Application:** A small cafe must register with the competent authority, maintain appropriate hygiene controls, train staff and operate procedures proportionate to its activities.
4. **Conclusion:** Reg 852/2004 is the baseline hygiene regulation: it shifts compliance from prescriptive commodity rules to outcome-focused FBO responsibility.

### Key references to score extra marks
- Regulation (EC) No 852/2004, Articles 4, 5 and 6
- Regulation (EC) No 852/2004, Annex I and Annex II
- Regulation (EC) No 853/2004
- Regulation (EU) 2017/625

## Q2. HACCP obligations and flexibility

**Topic area:** HACCP
**Difficulty:** core / applied
**Time budget:** 1 hour

### Question prompt

> Discuss the HACCP requirement under Article 5 of Regulation (EC) No 852/2004. What are the seven HACCP principles, and how does flexibility apply to small or low-risk food businesses?

### Answer outline

1. **Opening:** Cite Article 5.1: FBOs must put in place, implement and maintain permanent procedures based on HACCP principles. Primary producers and associated operations are excepted.
2. **Main body - seven Codex principles in Article 5.2:**
   - Identify hazards that must be prevented, eliminated or reduced.
   - Identify critical control points.
   - Establish critical limits.
   - Establish monitoring procedures.
   - Establish corrective actions.
   - Establish verification procedures.
   - Establish documents and records proportionate to the nature and size of the business.
3. **Flexibility:**
   - Legal basis: Article 5 and Recital 15 of Reg 852/2004.
   - Option 1: good hygiene practices/prerequisites alone may control hazards in low-risk activities.
   - Option 2: use a recognised guide to good practice, such as I.S. 340 in Ireland.
   - Option 3: develop a full FSMS based on the classical HACCP approach.
   - FSAI Guidance Note 11 provides a framework for assessing HACCP compliance.
4. **Application:** A sandwich manufacturer needs a more formal HACCP plan than a low-risk retailer reheating prepacked food; both must still show hazard control.
5. **Conclusion:** HACCP is mandatory, but the legal test is proportionate control of hazards, not identical paperwork for every FBO.

### Key references to score extra marks
- Regulation (EC) No 852/2004, Article 5
- Regulation (EC) No 852/2004, Recital 15
- FSAI Guidance Note 11
- Codex HACCP principles
- I.S. 340

## Q3. Food safety culture and running a food business

**Topic area:** Running a Food Business
**Difficulty:** applied / debate
**Time budget:** 1 hour

### Question prompt

> You are advising a new food business operator in Ireland. Outline the key legal steps required to operate lawfully and safely, including registration, food safety management, training, traceability and food safety culture.

### Answer outline

1. **Opening:** Use the lecturer's five-step framework: registration/approval; FSMS; traceability and risk analysis; training; compliance with food laws.
2. **Main body:**
   - **Registration/approval:** Article 6 of Reg 852/2004. Most FBOs register with the HSE; businesses handling foods of animal origin may require DAFM approval.
   - **FSMS and HACCP:** Article 5 requires procedures based on HACCP principles. Prerequisite hygiene controls under Annex II support HACCP.
   - **Traceability:** Reg 178/2002 Article 18 requires one-step-back and one-step-forward traceability; Article 19 governs withdrawal/recall.
   - **Training:** Annex II Chapter XII requires food handlers to be supervised, instructed and/or trained in food hygiene; HACCP-responsible staff need adequate HACCP training.
   - **Food Safety Culture:** mandatory since March 2021 through Reg (EU) 2021/382 amending Reg 852/2004; includes management commitment, communication, employee awareness and continuous improvement.
   - **Specific laws:** labelling under Reg 1169/2011, allergens under Annex II of FIC, microbiological criteria where relevant, and claims rules if marketing claims are used.
3. **Application:** For a chilled ready-meal start-up, mention registration/approval check, HACCP for cooking/cooling, allergen controls, supplier traceability, temperature records and recall procedure.
4. **Conclusion:** Lawful operation is an integrated system, not a single licence: the FBO must be able to demonstrate control before, during and after production.

### Key references to score extra marks
- Regulation (EC) No 852/2004, Articles 5 and 6, Annex II
- Regulation (EU) 2021/382
- Regulation (EC) No 178/2002, Articles 18 and 19
- Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011, Article 21 and Annex II
- FSAI Guidance Note 10 and Guidance Note 11
