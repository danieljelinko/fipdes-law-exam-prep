## Q1. The framework for nutrition and health claims

**Topic area:** Nutrition & Health Claims
**Difficulty:** core (explanatory)
**Time budget:** 1 hour

### Question prompt

> Outline the EU framework governing nutrition and health claims on food labels. Distinguish between nutrition claims and health claims, and explain the role of EFSA and the EU Register.

### Answer outline

1. **Opening:** Cite Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006 as the controlling regulation for nutrition and health claims made in commercial communications, labelling, presentation and advertising.
2. **Main body:**
   - **Nutrition claim:** states, suggests or implies beneficial nutritional properties, such as "source of fibre", "low fat" or "high protein". Only claims listed in the Annex to Reg 1924/2006 may be used, subject to conditions.
   - **Health claim:** states, suggests or implies a relationship between a food/category/constituent and health.
   - **Article 13.1 claims:** general function claims based on generally accepted scientific evidence, e.g. nutrient contribution to normal body function.
   - **Article 13.5 claims:** health claims based on newly developed scientific evidence or requests for proprietary data protection.
   - **Article 14 claims:** reduction-of-disease-risk claims and claims referring to children's development and health.
   - EFSA assesses scientific substantiation; the Commission and Member States decide authorisation and listing.
3. **Application:** Use examples: "source of vitamin C" as a nutrition claim; oat grain fibre contributing to increased faecal bulk as an Article 13 claim; calcium reducing bone mineral loss in post-menopausal women as an Article 14 reduction-of-disease-risk claim.
4. **Conclusion:** The framework is evidence-based and harmonised, but only works if claims are used with exact conditions of use and are not allowed to imply a generally unhealthy food is healthy.

### Key references to score extra marks
- Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006
- Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006, Articles 13.1, 13.5 and 14
- Regulation (EU) No 432/2012 (permitted Article 13.1 health claims list)
- EU Register of nutrition and health claims

## Q2. Mandatory nutrition declaration and claim conditions

**Topic area:** Nutrition declaration and claims
**Difficulty:** core / applied
**Time budget:** 1 hour

### Question prompt

> Explain how the mandatory nutrition declaration under Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 supports the regulation of nutrition and health claims. In your answer, discuss "source of" and "high in" claims and the importance of Reference Intakes.

### Answer outline

1. **Opening:** Link FIC and claims law: Reg 1169/2011 sets the mandatory nutrition declaration, while Reg 1924/2006 controls whether claims may be made.
2. **Main body:**
   - **Article 30 of Reg 1169/2011** requires the mandatory seven-part declaration: energy, fat, saturates, carbohydrate, sugars, protein and salt.
   - Information must normally be given per 100 g or 100 ml and in the required order; supplementary values per portion may also be used.
   - Vitamins and minerals must be listed in Annex XIII, present in significant amounts, and declared in specified units with percentage Reference Intake.
   - "Source of" vitamins/minerals generally requires at least **15% RI per 100 g/100 ml** or **7.5% RI per 100 ml for beverages**.
   - "High in" generally requires at least **30% RI**.
3. **Application:** A drink claiming to be "source of vitamin C" must meet the beverage threshold and show the relevant vitamin amount and % RI. If a claim concerns a nutrient not in the mandatory seven, the amount must be declared close to the nutrition table.
4. **Conclusion:** The nutrition declaration turns claims into checkable legal statements; without numeric declaration and RI conditions, claims would be marketing language rather than verifiable information.

### Key references to score extra marks
- Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011, Article 30
- Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011, Annex XIII
- Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006, Annex

## Q3. Are nutrition and health claims helpful to consumers?

**Topic area:** Nutrition & Health Claims
**Difficulty:** debate
**Time budget:** 1 hour

### Question prompt

> "Nutrition and health claims help consumers make healthier choices." Critically discuss this statement with reference to Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006, EFSA assessment and the risk that claims may mislead consumers.

### Answer outline

1. **Opening:** State both sides: claims can translate science into consumer information, but can also create a health halo around foods high in fat, sugar or salt.
2. **Main body - arguments that claims help:**
   - Harmonised rules prevent arbitrary national approaches and create legal certainty for FBOs.
   - EFSA scientific assessment improves reliability of authorised health claims.
   - EU Register and Reg 432/2012 make permitted claims and conditions transparent.
   - Conditions of use force precision, e.g. calcium claim for reduced bone mineral loss requires the stated target group and intake conditions.
3. **Main body - arguments that claims can mislead:**
   - Consumers may not understand the difference between a nutrition claim and a health claim.
   - A product high in sugar/salt/fat may carry a positive claim such as "low fat" or "source of vitamin D".
   - Nutrient profiles were intended under Reg 1924/2006 to prevent claims masking a poor overall nutritional profile, but EU-wide nutrient profiles have still not been set.
   - Some terms, such as "probiotic", are treated as health claims, and there are no approved probiotic health claims.
4. **Conclusion:** Claims are useful where they are authorised, specific and contextualised by the nutrition declaration; they are less effective where consumers treat a single positive claim as a proxy for overall healthiness.

### Key references to score extra marks
- Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006
- Regulation (EU) No 432/2012
- Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011, Article 7 and Article 30
- EU Register of nutrition and health claims
