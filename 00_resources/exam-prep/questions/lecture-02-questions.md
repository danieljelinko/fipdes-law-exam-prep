## Q1. Is Reg (EC) 178/2002 fit for purpose?

**Topic area:** General Food Law — evaluation
**Difficulty:** debate
**Time budget:** 1 hour

### Question prompt

> "Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 was designed in response to the food crises of the 1990s. Critically discuss whether it remains fit for purpose more than two decades later, drawing on specific articles and at least two named crises."

### Answer outline

1. **Opening:** State the three objectives of 178/2002 (slide 45 / Art 1):
   - Common basis for food law in MS
   - Establish EFSA + procedures for food safety
   - High level of consumer protection (also animal welfare, plant health, environment).
2. **Main body — strengths:**
   - Established **EFSA** as independent risk-assessment body (Art 22).
   - **Article 14** — clear definition of "unsafe food" (injurious or unfit).
   - **Article 18** + **Article 19** — traceability and recall obligations.
   - **RASFF** — rapid alert system for food and feed.
3. **Main body — weaknesses / open questions:**
   - Focus on "safe food" vs nutritionally deficient, sustainable, marketed-misleading food (MacMaolain 2019).
   - Risk analysis still split between EFSA (assessment) and Commission/MS (management) — slow under crisis.
   - Fenugreek seeds E. coli (2011) revealed gaps in cross-border traceability.
   - Horsemeat scandal (2013) revealed adulteration/fraud rather than safety per se — 178/2002 less suited.
4. **Conclusion:** Fit for purpose for safety, less so for fraud, sustainability, and nutrition policy. Reform pressure visible in Farm to Fork Strategy and 1169/2011 revision proposal.

### Key references to score extra marks
- Reg (EC) No 178/2002, Articles 1, 2, 14, 18, 19, 22
- BSE Inquiry Report (2001)
- MacMaolain (2019) *Irish Food Law*
- Fitness Check of the General Food Law (ec.europa.eu/food/safety/general_food_law/fitness_check_en)

## Q2. Article 14 — what makes food "unsafe"?

**Topic area:** General Food Law — unsafe food
**Difficulty:** core (explanatory)
**Time budget:** 1 hour

### Question prompt

> Define "unsafe food" under Regulation (EC) No 178/2002. What considerations apply in determining whether food is injurious to health or unfit for human consumption? Illustrate with examples.

### Answer outline

1. **Opening:** Cite Art 14.1 — "Food shall not be placed on the market if it is unsafe." Define "placing on the market" via Art 3.8.
2. **Main body — the unsafe test:**
   - **Art 14.2** — unsafe if (a) injurious to health or (b) unfit for human consumption.
   - **Art 14.3** — consider: normal conditions of use; stage of production/processing/distribution; information provided to consumer (including labelling, e.g. allergens).
   - **Art 14.4** — consider probable immediate/short-term/long-term effects on consumer, subsequent generations, cumulative toxic effects, sensitivities of specific consumer categories.
   - **Art 14.5** — "unfit" includes contamination, putrefaction, deterioration, decay.
3. **Examples:** allergen-mislabelled product (injurious to allergic consumer); rotten meat (unfit); cumulative pesticide residue (long-term harm).
4. **Conclusion:** Definition is deliberately broad — flexible to cover new hazards (cumulative chemicals, novel allergens).

### Key references to score extra marks
- Reg (EC) No 178/2002, Article 14 (parts 1–5), Article 3.8
- FSAI Guidance Note 10 (recall/traceability)
- Iceland Vegetable Lasagne FSAI alert example (plastic/rubber contamination — slide 106)

## Q3. Article 7 — the Precautionary Principle

**Topic area:** Risk analysis — precautionary principle
**Difficulty:** debate
**Time budget:** 1 hour

### Question prompt

> Explain the Precautionary Principle as set out in Article 7 of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002. When may it be invoked, and what are the legal limits? Critically assess its application with reference to a contemporary case.

### Answer outline

1. **Opening:** Cite Art 7 — provisional risk-management measures may be adopted *"where, following an assessment of available information, the possibility of harmful effects on health is identified but scientific uncertainty persists"*.
2. **Main body — conditions:**
   - Risk assessment must have been done (so it is **not** a substitute for science).
   - Measures are **provisional** — pending further scientific evidence.
   - Must be proportionate and non-discriminatory.
   - Reviewable as new evidence emerges.
3. **Application example — glyphosate/Roundup (slides 343–345):** EU + US regulators consider glyphosate not likely carcinogenic; WHO IARC classifies "probably carcinogenic" (2015). EU re-authorisation debates illustrate precautionary tension.
4. **Counter-uses:** GMOs (EU stricter than US); BPA, neonicotinoid pesticides; hormones in beef.
5. **Conclusion:** Strength = enables protective action under uncertainty; weakness = can be politicised, can over-restrict in absence of evidence (Codex/WTO tensions).

### Key references to score extra marks
- Reg (EC) No 178/2002, Article 7
- EU Communication on the Precautionary Principle (2000) — eur-lex.europa.eu CELEX:52000DC0001
- WHO IARC glyphosate classification (2015)
- WTO SPS Agreement compatibility

## Q4. The role of EFSA — assessment vs management

**Topic area:** EFSA & FSAI
**Difficulty:** core (explanatory + comparative)
**Time budget:** 1 hour

### Question prompt

> Discuss the role of the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) under Regulation (EC) No 178/2002. Why is the separation between risk assessment and risk management considered essential, and how does this distinction play out in practice?

### Answer outline

1. **Opening:** EFSA established by **Article 22** of Reg 178/2002. Located in Parma, Italy. Independent agency funded by EU.
2. **Main body — EFSA's role (Art 22.2–22.7):**
   - **Risk assessment** — scientific opinions to support EU legislation (food/feed safety).
   - **Risk communication** — to public and stakeholders.
   - **NOT risk management** — that sits with **Commission** and **Member States**.
   - Scientific Committee and Panels deliver opinions, published in *EFSA Journal*.
3. **Why separation matters:**
   - **BSE lesson:** the 2000 BSE Inquiry Report identified failure of **risk communication** and lack of transparency in early risk assessment (Southwood Working Party) — separation is the structural fix.
   - Protects scientific independence from political pressure.
   - Builds public trust.
4. **Practical example:** EFSA's opinions on health claims (Reg 1924/2006) — EFSA assesses scientific substantiation; Commission decides legislative authorisation; MS enforce.
5. **Conclusion:** Separation is a constitutional principle of EU food governance — essential for credibility but creates coordination challenges in fast-moving crises.

### Key references to score extra marks
- Reg (EC) No 178/2002, Article 22.2, 22.3, 22.4, 22.6, 22.7
- Article 23 — EFSA's tasks
- BSE Inquiry Report (2001) — slide 49
- EFSA values: openness/transparency, excellence in science, independence, responsiveness

## Q5. Compare EFSA and FSAI

**Topic area:** EFSA & FSAI
**Difficulty:** comparative
**Time budget:** 1 hour

### Question prompt

> Compare and contrast the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) and the Food Safety Authority of Ireland (FSAI). How do their roles, governance, and relationship to risk analysis differ, and how do they coordinate?

### Answer outline

1. **Opening:** Both authorities are **independent**, **science-led** bodies in food safety governance. EFSA = EU-level (Art 22 Reg 178/2002). FSAI = Irish national body, established by **FSAI Act 1998**.
2. **Main body — comparison:**
   | Feature | EFSA | FSAI |
   |---|---|---|
   | Established | Reg 178/2002 Art 22 (2002) | FSAI Act 1998 |
   | Scope | EU-wide; advises Commission/EP/MS | Ireland; advises DoH; coordinates HSE, DAFM, SFPA, Local Authorities |
   | Risk roles | Assessment + Communication (NOT management) | Risk assessment + communication + service contracts with enforcement bodies |
   | Outputs | EFSA Journal (monthly, open-access) | FSAI Guidance Notes, alerts, scientific recommendations |
3. **Coordination:**
   - FSAI represents Ireland on EFSA forums.
   - FSAI's qualitative risk assessment aligned with EFSA methodology.
   - National implementation under S.I. No. 22 of 2020 (DAFM-supervised) and S.I. No. 747 of 2007 (HSE-supervised) — transposing Reg 178/2002 into Irish law.
4. **Examples:** Iceland Vegetable Lasagne alert (FSAI); EFSA pesticide re-evaluations affecting Irish market.
5. **Conclusion:** Complementary: EFSA gives the EU-level science; FSAI translates and enforces in Ireland. Independence preserved at both levels.

### Key references to score extra marks
- Reg (EC) No 178/2002, Article 22
- FSAI Act 1998
- S.I. No. 22 of 2020; S.I. No. 747 of 2007
- Slide 76–77 — FSAI's qualitative risk-assessment process

## Q6. Article 18 — Traceability obligations

**Topic area:** Traceability & Risk Analysis
**Difficulty:** core (explanatory + applied)
**Time budget:** 1 hour

### Question prompt

> Explain the traceability obligations on food business operators under Article 18 of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002. Distinguish between "one step back" and "one step forward" traceability, and describe the FSAI's guidance on developing a traceability system.

### Answer outline

1. **Opening:** Define traceability (Reg 178/2002 Art 3) — *"the ability to trace and follow a food, feed, food-producing animal or substance… through all stages of production, processing and distribution."*
2. **Main body:**
   - **Art 18.1** — traceability established at all stages.
   - **Art 18.2** — FBOs identify any person from whom they have been supplied (**"one step back"** — LEGAL).
   - **Art 18.3** — info available to competent authorities on demand.
   - **Art 18.4** — FBOs identify other businesses to which their products have been supplied (**"one step forward"** — LEGAL, except where final consumer).
   - **Process/internal traceability** — best practice but not legally required.
3. **FSAI 6-step framework (slide 101):**
   - Define scope; decide batch size; identify info needed (raw materials, internal, distribution); record keeping and retrieval system; review and test the system; document the system.
4. **Record retention:** S.I. 747 of 2007 → all food businesses, approx 6 months to 5 years (HSE-supervised); S.I. 432 of 2009 → 3 years for foods of animal origin (DAFM).
5. **Conclusion:** Traceability is the operational backbone for recall/withdrawal (Art 19); a small/narrow batch + detailed traceability = smaller recall.

### Key references to score extra marks
- Reg (EC) No 178/2002, Articles 2 (def), 3 (def), 18 (obligation), 19 (withdrawal/recall)
- FSAI Guidance Note 10 (Product Recall and Traceability, Rev 3)
- S.I. No. 747 of 2007; S.I. No. 432 of 2009
- Directive 2011/91/EU (lot/batch marking)

## Q7. Withdrawal versus Recall — a scenario question

**Topic area:** Risk Analysis — unsafe food response
**Difficulty:** applied scenario
**Time budget:** 1 hour

### Question prompt

> You are the food safety manager at a manufacturer of ready-to-eat sandwich products. Routine testing reveals Listeria monocytogenes in a batch of egg-mayonnaise sandwiches, half of which have already been distributed to retail. Outline the steps you must take, citing the relevant articles and FSAI guidance.

### Answer outline

1. **Opening:** Frame in terms of Reg 178/2002 Article 14 (unsafe food) and Article 19 (FBO obligations on withdrawal/recall). Cite FSAI Guidance Note 10.
2. **Main body — decision tree (FSAI GN10 Appendix 1):**
   - Suspect non-compliance → **suspend distribution**.
   - Assemble **food incident team**.
   - Collate product/production/QA information.
   - Determine whether food is **unsafe** (Appendix 2 + risk assessment Appendix 3).
   - Outcome paths:
     - Unsafe + may have reached consumer → **RECALL** + notify competent authorities + notify trade customers + notify consumers (in-store notice, press release).
     - Unsafe + has not reached consumer → **WITHDRAWAL** + notify competent authorities + trade customers.
     - Not unsafe but not compliant → consult competent authorities.
     - Not unsafe and compliant → monitor as complaint.
3. **Specifics for this scenario:** Listeria = injurious to health (esp. pregnant women, immunocompromised). Half-distributed = some may have reached consumer → **RECALL**. Use trade notification form (URGENT — Food Recall) and in-store/press notification (FSAI GN10 App 4 templates).
4. **Batch logic:** Apply Art 14.6 / Reg 2073/2005 — assume **all** food in that batch is unsafe unless evidence to the contrary.
5. **Conclusion:** Speed of response + completeness of documentation determine both regulatory compliance and reputational damage.

### Key references to score extra marks
- Reg (EC) No 178/2002, Articles 14, 18, 19
- Reg (EC) No 2073/2005 (microbiological criteria for foodstuffs)
- FSAI Guidance Note 10, Appendices 1–4
- Sample press-release format (slide 116 — Ham-It-Up Salmonella case)

## Q8. Article 8 — protection of consumers' interests

**Topic area:** General Food Law — consumer protection
**Difficulty:** debate / explanatory
**Time budget:** 1 hour

### Question prompt

> Discuss Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002. How does it complement the specific provisions on misleading labelling under Article 7 of Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011? Use examples to illustrate.

### Answer outline

1. **Opening:** Art 8 of Reg 178/2002 — *"Food law shall aim at the protection of the interests of consumers"* and provide a basis for **informed choices**. Aimed at preventing (a) fraudulent or deceptive practices; (b) adulteration of food; (c) any other practices which may mislead the consumer.
2. **Relationship with Reg 1169/2011 Art 7:**
   - 178/2002 Art 8 is the **general principle**; 1169/2011 Art 7 is the **operationalisation** in labelling.
   - 1169/2011 Art 7 lists specific misleading practices: characteristics, effects/properties food doesn't possess, special characteristics shared by all similar foods (e.g. "oranges containing Vitamin C"), substitution by appearance.
3. **Examples:**
   - Horsemeat scandal (2013) — adulteration breach under Art 8.
   - Olive oil fraud — fraudulent practice under Art 8.
   - "100% natural" / "artisan" marketing — covered by Art 7 of 1169/2011.
4. **Conclusion:** The two articles together prohibit a wide spectrum from outright fraud (Art 8 of 178/2002) to subtle visual misleadingness (Art 7 of 1169/2011). Enforcement remains the practical gap.

### Key references to score extra marks
- Reg (EC) No 178/2002, Article 8
- Reg (EU) No 1169/2011, Article 7
- Horsemeat scandal (2013); Olive oil fraud cases
- FSAI Guidance Document 29 (Marketing terms: artisan/farmhouse/traditional/natural)
