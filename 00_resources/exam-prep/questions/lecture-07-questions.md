## Q1. Scope and purpose of Regulation 853/2004

**Topic area:** Foods of Animal Origin
**Difficulty:** core (explanatory)
**Time budget:** 1 hour

### Question prompt

> Explain the scope and purpose of Regulation (EC) No 853/2004. How does it relate to Regulation (EC) No 852/2004, and which food businesses are most affected?

### Answer outline

1. **Opening:** Reg 853/2004 lays down specific hygiene rules for food of animal origin. It applies in addition to Reg 852/2004, not instead of it.
2. **Main body:**
   - Reg 852/2004 sets general hygiene rules for all FBOs; Reg 853/2004 adds product-specific and establishment-specific rules for foods of animal origin.
   - "Products of animal origin" include food of animal origin, including honey and blood, live bivalve molluscs, live echinoderms, tunicates, marine gastropods and other animals intended for supply to the final consumer.
   - Annex III contains specific requirements for sectors such as meat, poultry, minced meat, meat preparations, meat products, fishery products, raw milk, dairy products, eggs, rendered animal fats, gelatin and collagen.
   - Composite foods may fall outside direct 853 approval in some retail contexts, but unprocessed animal-origin ingredients used in them must comply with 853 requirements.
3. **Application:** A dairy processor, slaughterhouse or fish processor will normally face 853 approval and marking obligations; a cafe using approved ingredients normally remains primarily under 852 unless it undertakes covered wholesale animal-origin activities.
4. **Conclusion:** Reg 853/2004 reflects higher public-health risks and supply-chain controls for animal-origin foods.

- Regulation (EC) No 852/2004
- Regulation (EC) No 853/2004
- Regulation (EC) No 853/2004, Annex I and Annex III
- S.I. No. 22 of 2020; S.I. No. 432 of 2009

## Q2. Approval, health marking and identification marking

**Topic area:** Foods of Animal Origin
**Difficulty:** core / applied
**Time budget:** 1 hour

### Question prompt

> Discuss the approval and marking obligations for establishments handling foods of animal origin under Regulation (EC) No 853/2004. Distinguish between registration, approval, health marks and identification marks.

### Answer outline

1. **Opening:** Food businesses subject to Reg 853/2004 usually require approval by the competent authority before placing products of animal origin on the market.
2. **Main body:**
   - **Registration** under Reg 852/2004 Article 6 identifies the establishment and activity for official controls; it is not the same as prior approval.
   - **Approval** is required for establishments handling products for which Reg 853/2004 Annex III lays down requirements, unless an exemption or retail derogation applies.
   - In Ireland, DAFM is central for many animal-origin establishments; national implementation includes S.I. No. 22 of 2020 and S.I. No. 432 of 2009.
   - Products of animal origin from approved establishments cannot be placed on the market unless a health mark or identification mark is applied.
   - **Health mark:** used for fresh meat and applied under official veterinary responsibility.
   - **Identification mark:** required for other products of animal origin; the oval mark includes the country and establishment approval number.
3. **Application:** A cheese plant must be approved and apply an identification mark to products; a slaughterhouse must satisfy approval and fresh meat health-marking requirements.
4. **Conclusion:** Approval and marking make official control and traceability visible on the product itself.

### Key references to score extra marks
- Regulation (EC) No 852/2004, Article 6
- Regulation (EC) No 853/2004, Annex II and Annex III
- Regulation (EU) 2017/625
- Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/627

## Q3. Official controls and flexibility for animal-origin foods

**Topic area:** Hygiene Package and official controls
**Difficulty:** debate / applied
**Time budget:** 1 hour

### Question prompt

> Critically discuss whether the EU rules on foods of animal origin strike the right balance between public-health protection and flexibility for small food businesses.

### Answer outline

1. **Opening:** State the tension: animal-origin foods carry higher microbiological and zoonotic risks, but overly rigid rules can burden small/local producers.
2. **Main body - protection rationale:**
   - Reg 853/2004 adds specific controls to the baseline of Reg 852/2004.
   - Official controls originally sat under Reg 854/2004, now replaced by Reg (EU) 2017/625 from 14 December 2019.
   - Slaughter controls include ante-mortem and post-mortem inspection requirements.
   - Cold-chain and product-specific controls reduce risk in meat, milk, fishery products and eggs.
   - Health and identification marks support traceability and enforcement.
3. **Main body - flexibility rationale:**
   - Reg 853/2004 contains derogations and flexibility provisions, including Article 10.
   - Retail, marginal, localised and restricted activities may sometimes remain under Reg 852/2004 rather than full 853 approval.
   - Flexibility is important for small establishments, local food systems and traditional products, provided food-safety objectives are met.
4. **Application:** A small butcher supplying local retail may need a different regulatory route from a national meat processor supplying other establishments across Member States.
5. **Conclusion:** The framework is defensible if flexibility is evidence-based and does not dilute the core controls: approval where required, official inspection, cold chain, traceability and marking.

### Key references to score extra marks
- Regulation (EC) No 853/2004, Article 10
- Regulation (EC) No 852/2004
- Regulation (EU) 2017/625
- Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2019/624
- Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/627
