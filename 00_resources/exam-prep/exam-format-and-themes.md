## 2. What gets extra marks — *read this twice*

From slide 543:

> **References to legislation = extra marks.**

From slide 542 (post-assignment feedback):

> **"KNOWLEDGE AND REFERENCES MISSING"** — was the main gap in labelling assignments.

**Translation for the exam:**

- Cite **regulation number + year + scope** whenever you can.
- Cite **article numbers** where relevant (especially Reg 178/2002 Articles 5, 6, 7, 8, 14, 18, 19, 22).
- Cite **named cases** (BSE, Dioxin, Horsemeat, Fenugreek seeds E. coli, Roundup/glyphosate, Sudan 1).
- Reference **FSAI Guidance Notes** by number (especially GN 10 — recall/traceability, GN 11 — HACCP, GN 17 — meat labelling).
- Use **specific dates** when describing legal-history changes (e.g. *"Reg (EC) 178/2002 of 28 January 2002"*; *"Food Safety Culture became mandatory under amended 852/2004 in March 2021"*).

## 4. Lecturer's explicit debate prompts *(slide 545, verbatim)*

These read like the lecturer's hints — phrased the way an exam question would phrase them. Prepare a position on **each**.

1. **Food Information — where it can be misleading.**
2. **Misleading information & plant-based terms:** *Can we use terms like "milk", "sausage", "steak" for plant-based products?* — debate from perspective of the **meat lobby** vs **vegetarian/vegan** advocates.
3. **Misleading information & greenwashing:** debate how a label can indicate sustainability — what is and isn't allowed.
4. **Nutrition & health claims:** debate whether they are **helpful to consumers** — consider clarity, evidence base, EFSA opinions, "function" vs "reduction of disease risk" claims, the criticism that they let unhealthy foods masquerade as healthy.
5. **Sustainability in food labelling** — see L5 + the Farm-to-Fork strategy.
6. **The General Food Law — is it fit for purpose?** Refer back to BSE, dioxin and other crises.

## 7. "Running a Food Business" — the 5-step framework *(slide 550)*

A **structural answer template** the lecturer uses repeatedly. If a question asks "how do you comply with food law" or "walk through what an FBO must do", structure your answer around these five steps:

1. **Registration & Approval** — Reg 852/2004 Art 6 (HSE/DAFM)
2. **Food Safety Management System (FSMS)** — including the mandatory **Food Safety Culture** (since March 2021, Reg 2021/382)
3. **Traceability & Risk Analysis** — Reg 178/2002 Art 6 + Art 18; FSAI GN 10
4. **Food Safety Training** — for all food handlers
5. **Comply with food laws** — microbial growth (Reg 2073/2005), labelling (Reg 1169/2011), claims (Reg 1924/2006), voluntary labelling (organic / gluten-free / sustainability)

## 8. Cross-cutting themes — the deck keeps returning to these

These show up in **multiple lectures** and are likely to be combined in a single exam question:

- **Misleading information / consumer protection** — Reg 178/2002 Art 8 + Reg 1169/2011 Art 7 (overlap is intentional)
- **Precautionary principle & risk analysis** — Reg 178/2002 Art 6, Art 7; EFSA's risk-assessment role; assessment ≠ management
- **Traceability & recall/withdrawal** — Reg 178/2002 Art 18 + 19; "one step back, one step forward"; FSAI GN 10
- **Food Safety Culture** *(NEW since 2021)* — mandatory under amended 852/2004
- **Sustainability / greenwashing / voluntary claims** — Farm to Fork Strategy; organic vs free-from vs sustainable claims
- **Allergen management** — 14 allergens in Annex II of FIC 1169/2011; emphasis in ingredients list; prepacked vs non-prepacked
- **EFSA independence** — Reg 178/2002 Art 22; EFSA does **risk assessment + risk communication** but **NOT** risk management (which sits with Commission + Member States)
- **Free movement of goods vs national food rules** — EU institutions, member-state competence, "race to the bottom" critique

## 9. How to use this with practice questions

There are **two directories** of exam material:

- `exam-prep/` — study material (this summary, the question files with answer outlines, and the reusable active-recall template).
- `exam-simulation/` — **ready-to-paste ChatGPT prompts**, one per lecture, pre-filled with the lecture content + questions. No assembly required.

**Recommended workflow:**

1. **Read this summary** through once to anchor the topic map in your head.
2. **Open the practice-question file** for a lecture: `exam-prep/questions/lecture-NN-questions.md`. Skim the questions — but don't read the outlines yet.
3. **Run an active-recall session:** open `exam-simulation/lecture-NN-active-recall.md`, copy its entire content, and paste it into a fresh ChatGPT / Claude chat. The LLM will ask you questions one at a time and grade your recall.
4. **After the session**, read the outlines in the question file to see what you missed.
5. **Cycle through all 7 content lectures**, then attempt the cross-cutting questions.

## 10. Quick exam-day reminders

- **You have 3 hours for 3 essays.** Spend ≤ 10 minutes planning each answer, ≤ 45 minutes writing, ≤ 5 minutes reviewing for missing regulation references.
- **Open every answer by citing the controlling regulation.** *e.g. "This question concerns the General Food Law, Regulation (EC) No 178/2002, in particular Article 14 on unsafe food…"*
- **Close every answer with an evaluation.** Don't just describe — say whether the framework is **fit for purpose**, what could be improved, and why.
- **If you don't know an answer cold, pick the question that lets you cite the most regulations.** That's where the extra marks live.
